 The fact that the **brand new tube is already developing a black mark on one end**, combined with the **clicking/crackling noise when you twist it**, points away from the tombstones and directly toward either a **failing magnetic ballast** or a **bad starter socket connection** dumping excess electrical current into the tube filaments.

Here is why your new tube is blackening and why twisting makes it work:

1. **Why one end is turning black:** A fluorescent tube turns black at the ends when it gets overheated or receives an incorrect voltage surge (usually caused by a shorted ballast or a stuck starter failing to open/close properly).
2. **Why twisting helps:** When you twist the tube, you are shifting the pins slightly inside the socket. This temporarily forces a weak or oxidized electrical connection to make solid contact, allowing the weak surge from the ballast to finally arc across and light the gas.
3. **The 30-minute cooldown rule:** When the light is on, components heat up and metal expands slightly, maintaining connection. After 30 minutes off, everything cools down and contracts, breaking the weak electrical path or causing high resistance so the ballast cannot ignite the tube.

### Revised Troubleshooting Steps (Check Before Changing Sockets)

1. **Inspect the Starter Again:** Critical Check.
Since you have a T12 fixture, the **starter cylinder** is responsible for pre-heating the tube filaments. If the starter is stuck or failing, it keeps feeding too much voltage into one end of the tube, turning it black and failing to ignite it when cold. Have you replaced the starter cylinder yet?

* **Verification:** If the starter is old, replace it immediately before buying new sockets. A bad starter will instantly ruin a brand new fluorescent tube.


2. **Feel for Heat and Listen for Humming:** Check the Ballast.
Turn the light on and let it run, then check the metal fixture housing near the ballast. If the ballast is excessively hot to the touch, smells slightly like warm varnish or electrical ozone, or hums loudly, the ballast is failing and cannot provide the steady starting voltage.

* **Verification:** A failing ballast requires either replacing the ballast or bypassing it completely to convert to LED.


Given that fluorescent ballasts and replacement tubes cost money and T12 technology is becoming obsolete, **this exact symptom is usually the tipping point where most homeowners decide to bypass the ballast and switch to a direct-wire LED tube.**

That confirms it: **your magnetic ballast is failing.**

That audible humming noise means the internal copper coils and core of the ballast are vibrating under stress, and it is failing to deliver the steady electrical voltage needed to properly ionize the gas in the tube when cold. This is also why it overheated and started blackening one end of your brand new tube.

At this point, replacing just the ballast for an old 24-inch T12 fixture can be hard to find and often costs nearly as much as a new fixture or an LED conversion kit.

### Your Best Options Moving Forward

1. **Convert to Direct-Wire LED (Bypass Ballast):** Option 1 (Recommended).
Remove the failing magnetic ballast and the starter entirely, then wire the incoming power straight to the sockets. This eliminates the humming, stops burning out tubes, and saves energy.


2. **Replace the Entire Fixture:** Option 2.
Because 24-inch T12 fixtures and parts are becoming obsolete, buying a brand-new, modern integrated LED wrap-around or strip fixture (often under $25–$35) is frequently faster and cleaner than rewiring the old housing.
